# NI Signals SEO operating system

Prepared 8 September 2026. Goal: qualified discovery from suppliers looking for Northern Ireland projects and business changes, not traffic for its own sake.

## Search promise

Every report answers: what changed, who is involved, what suppliers could be relevant, how early or late the opportunity is, what is unverified and what to check next. Google recommends helpful, reliable, people-first content with original analysis, and warns against mass-produced search-first summaries. See https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

## Site architecture

- / — proposition and free briefing CTA
- /sample-briefing.html — five sourced examples and pilot
- /opportunities/ — index of reports
- /opportunities/[project].html — one page per material project
- /guides/how-to-find-commercial-opportunities-in-northern-ireland.html — cornerstone guide
- /guides/ni-planning-applications-for-suppliers.html — planning guide
- /guides/how-early-should-you-approach-a-commercial-project.html — timing guide
- /about.html — author, method, corrections and independence

Do not create thin pages for every town, company or keyword. Publish only when a page has distinct evidence and a useful answer.

## Report template

1. Descriptive title: [Project] — what changed and what suppliers should check.
2. One-sentence answer at the top.
3. Confirmed facts: organisation, location, announcement date, source and stage.
4. Why it may matter: supplier categories, labelled as NI Signals interpretation.
5. Timing: early, active, near opening or operational.
6. Procurement route: developer, operator, contractor, public tender or unknown.
7. What is not confirmed.
8. One practical next question.
9. Primary sources first, with checked dates.
10. Related reports and free briefing CTA.

Never copy an article. Add original selection, timing assessment and next action.

## 30-day cadence

Monday: one genuinely new or materially updated project report.
Wednesday: one durable guide based on a repeated supplier question.
Friday: one factual roundup linking to the strongest report. Skip filler.

Week 1: DHL, Roe Valley and Wall Group reports; planning and timing guides.
Week 2: SJH and Hollywood Bowl reports with stage caveats; new-premises checklist.
Week 3: Belfast, Antrim, Down, Mid Ulster and North West index.
Week 4: status updates, corrections and permissioned anonymised feedback.

## Distribution and measurement

Link each report from the sample or homepage when relevant, include it in the next email, share a factual LinkedIn excerpt, ask named organisations for corrections, and submit the sitemap in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. Google’s sitemap guidance: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap

Track weekly: indexed pages, impressions, clicks, query CTR, report visits, sample clicks, signups, qualified replies and pilot conversations. The leading metric is qualified actions per report.

## Technical baseline

Unique title and meta description; canonical URL; one descriptive H1; visible source, author/method and checked date; internal links; 200 response; robots.txt and XML sitemap. Add Article JSON-LD only when it matches visible article content. Keep static HTML fast and mobile-first.

## Compliance guardrail

Do not scrape personal profiles or treat public availability as blanket marketing permission. Screen recipient type, identify NI Signals, provide an opt-out route and keep suppression records. ICO guidance: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
